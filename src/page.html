What this is

A single-page, dependency-free timeline of deep time, built around one commitment: uncertainty is drawn, not hidden. "No data" is shown differently from zero, disputed categories are hatched, and every value says where a real figure would come from.

This is an early working version. Much of the data is now sourced, and what is not is marked as a placeholder. Geological dates now come from the International Chronostratigraphic Chart (v2024/12), and hominin species dates from the Smithsonian Human Origins Program, with Denisovans, H. antecessor and H. luzonensis from one dating paper each. The turning points on the Human time tab cite their sources too, except farming and first cities, which are still placeholders, and language, which no source dates. World population from 10,000 BCE to 2023 comes from Our World in Data's long-run series, which joins HYDE 3.3 (to 1790), Gapminder (1800–1949) and the UN World Population Prospects 2024 (from 1950). It gives a single figure per year with no range, so the line is dashed where the figures are model reconstructions (before 1800) and faded after that; the shaded range before 10,000 BCE is still a placeholder. On the Deep time tab, the climate row shows three records as separate strips, because they measure different things: deep-sea oxygen isotopes over 67 million years (CENOGRID, Westerhold et al. 2020; not a temperature), Antarctic temperature over 800,000 years (EPICA Dome C, Jouzel et al. 2007) and global temperature for the last 2,000 years (PAGES 2k, 2019). The carbon dioxide row shows individual proxy estimates (CenCO2PIP Consortium 2023, its categories 1 and 2 only), the Antarctic ice-core record (Bereiter et al. 2015) and Mauna Loa measurements from 1959. The life row counts the animal genera known from fossils in each geological stage, from the Paleobiology Database's own diversity tool. That count reflects how much has been found and recorded as well as how much life there was. The row also marks the five largest mass extinctions at the ICS dates of the boundaries where they are conventionally placed. Above the bars are three early milestones, each dated by one paper: signs of life in 3.48-billion-year-old hot-spring deposits (Djokic et al. 2017), the Great Oxidation Event (Gumsley et al. 2017) and the oldest multicellular eukaryotes (Miao et al. 2024). Below them, twelve living animal groups each show when their living members last shared a common ancestor, between the oldest fossil firmly in the group and the oldest plausible date (Benton et al. 2015). The largest-polity row shows, for every year from 3400 BCE, the polity with the largest mapped area in Cliopatria (Seshat Global History Databank, v0.2.1). It measures area on a map, not population, and Cliopatria notes that its borders are one version among disputed ones. The polity-population row next to it shows every population estimate in the Seshat Global History Databank (Polaris2026 release, CC BY-SA 4.0) as published, with its low and high figures. Seshat covers a sample of polities in 41 regions, not all of them, so the highest mark in a period is the largest polity in that sample. From 1950 a line shows the most populous country, from the UN World Population Prospects 2024. The energy row shows world energy use per person from 1800, calculated here by dividing Our World in Data's world energy use (Vaclav Smil's estimates before 1965, the Energy Institute's Statistical Review after) by its world population. It includes wood and other traditional fuels but not food, and before 1800 it shows no data. Which five species are labelled and which are drawn faintly is an editorial choice, not the source's. Every other number is still a placeholder written from memory and has not been checked against a source.

How certainty is drawn

Firm dates have hard edges
Uncertain ends fade across the plausible range
Species bars: a short fixed fade around the first and last fossil dates, because the source gives no range for them and species outlive their fossils; the fade's width is not data
Disputed category, or a scheme that does not apply
No data: hard to say, but some number for sure, so not zero
Dashed lines, faded lines and soft-topped bars: the source gives no uncertainty for these values, which is not the same as certain

Time base

All dates normalise to years before 2000 CE. "Now" is fixed at 2023, the last year in the population data, so "years ago" counts back from 2023. Source values carry explicit units (4.6 Ga, 11700 b2k, 3300 BCE, 1760 CE) and are converted in one place, so no row can silently disagree with another about what a date means.
