What this is

A single-page, dependency-free timeline of deep time, built around one commitment: uncertainty is drawn, not hidden. "No data" is shown differently from zero, disputed categories are hatched, and every value says where a real figure would come from.

This is an early working version. The structure is in place; most of the data is not. Geological dates now come from the International Chronostratigraphic Chart (v2024/12), and hominin species dates from the Smithsonian Human Origins Program (except Denisovans, H. antecessor and H. luzonensis). World population from 10,000 BCE to 2023 comes from Our World in Data's long-run series, which joins HYDE 3.3 (to 1790), Gapminder (1800–1949) and the UN World Population Prospects 2024 (from 1950). It gives a single figure per year with no range, so the line is dashed where the figures are model reconstructions (before 1800); the shaded range before 10,000 BCE is still a placeholder. Which five species are labelled and which are drawn faintly is an editorial choice, not the source's. Every other number is still a placeholder written from memory and has not been checked against a source.

How certainty is drawn

Firm dates have hard edges
Uncertain ends fade across the plausible range
Species bars: a short fixed fade past the first and last fossils, as a reminder that species outlive their fossils; the fade's width is not data
Disputed category, or a scheme that does not apply
No data, which is not the same as zero

Time base

All dates normalise to years before 2000 CE. Source values carry explicit units (4.6 Ga, 11700 b2k, 3300 BCE, 1760 CE) and are converted in one place, so no lane can silently disagree with another about what a date means.
